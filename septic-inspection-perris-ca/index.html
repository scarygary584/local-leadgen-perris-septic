Start with the reason for the inspection

A maintenance review, a property purchase, and investigation of a recurring fault may involve different deliverables. Tell the provider why the inspection is needed and whether another party has supplied a written checklist. Do not assume a routine pumping record meets a buyer, lender, or other recipient requirement.

Ask what the report includes

Confirm the components and accessible areas to be assessed, the methods proposed, and any limitations. Ask whether the result is a written condition report, photographs, service findings, or another document. Clarify whether pumping, tank locating, uncovering access, and any specialized evaluation are included or charged separately.

Gather records before the visit

Keep available diagrams, previous pumping invoices, inspection reports, repair records, and known system details together. Tell the provider if the tank location or size is unknown. Explain locked access, landscaping, and truck parking conditions. Leave covers and excavation to the provider; do not create access by digging near unknown buried services.

Coordinate timing and property access

For a transaction, identify the report recipient and the actual deadline before booking. Ask the provider whether it can supply the required documentation and discuss any recipient-specific conditions directly. A service request through this site does not guarantee a certificate, transaction approval, or that the system will pass an inspection.

Separate findings from repair proposals

Request a plain-language explanation of observed defects, inaccessible components, and further assessment recommendations. If repair work is proposed, ask for a separate written scope and quote. Confirm who is responsible for checking any applicable local requirements before alteration. Retain the report even if you obtain a second opinion.
